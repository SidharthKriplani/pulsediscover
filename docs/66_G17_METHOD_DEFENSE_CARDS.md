# 66 — G17: Method Defense Cards

*18 first-principles defense cards. Each: problem · intuition · mechanism · assumptions · why-here · why-not-alternatives · implementation evidence · numbers · failure modes · business-consequence-if-wrong · kill condition · senior trap · safe line · forbidden overclaim. All numbers are from prior gates (no new metrics).*

---

## 1. ALS f64 — warm collaborative retrieval
- **Problem:** rank items for users with interaction history. **Intuition:** users/items live in a low-rank latent space; similar tastes → nearby vectors. **Mechanism:** implicit-feedback matrix factorization (confidence-weighted ALS), score = ⟨user factor, item factor⟩, tuned to 64 dims. **Assumptions:** low-rank structure; implicit signal ≈ preference; warm items only (new items have no factor).
- **Why here:** strongest, cheapest, most reliable warm signal on this catalog. **Why not alternatives:** beat popularity (0.035), co-occ (0.05), and all sequence models tested. **Evidence:** `c2_als.pkl`, D3A+; **numbers:** R@20 **0.085** (5k LLO), best single generator. **Failure modes:** cold items (0 by construction), popularity bias, stale factors. **If wrong:** stale factors → warm relevance silently decays. **Kill:** if a generator beats it at equal eval on the domain. **Senior trap:** "why not deep models?" → they didn't win here. **Safe line:** "ALS f64 is my tuned warm floor (R@20 0.085)." **Forbidden:** "SOTA."

## 2. Content-hybrid — cold-start lane
- **Problem:** recommend brand-new items with no interactions. **Intuition:** new items resemble known ones via author/series/text. **Mechanism:** RRF of same-author + same-series + TF-IDF over cold items. **Assumptions:** content metadata predictive; new-author case is hardest.
- **Why here:** ALS is structurally 0 on new items; this is the only cold signal. **Why not alternatives:** pure popularity ignores personalization; two-tower-content not built. **Evidence:** C1 (`domain_coldstart_report.json`); **numbers:** cold R@20 **0.241**, top-100 lists surface all 1,420 cold items (catalog candidate coverage). **Failure modes:** weak on new authors (TF-IDF carries it), thin metadata. **If wrong:** bad cold picks erode trust. **Kill:** if cold recall ≈ 0 or quality complaints. **Senior trap:** "is that a cold-start *win*?" → no, a candidate channel. **Safe line:** "non-zero cold recall where CF is structurally zero." **Forbidden:** "cold-start solved."

## 3. Co-occurrence — candidate source (G15)
- **Problem:** add collaborative candidates ALS/SAS miss. **Intuition:** items read together are related. **Mechanism:** item-item co-counts (top-6k vocab, min-co 5), aggregate neighbors of history → top-100. **Assumptions:** basket co-occurrence ≈ relatedness; head-vocab cap loses tail.
- **Why here:** cheap CPU source, complementary structure. **Why not alternatives:** LightGCN not cheap (deferred). **Evidence:** G15 (`g15_candidate_source_report.json`); **numbers:** +3.76pp marginal coverage, 70% overlap, LTR R@20 +0.0177 (~1.5 SE). **Failure modes:** redundancy with ALS; popularity bias; vocab-cap blind spots. **If wrong:** pay candidate cost for redundant items. **Kill:** if marginal coverage ≈ 0 or no directional lift. **Senior trap:** "is it significant?" → no, conditional adopt. **Safe line:** "low-cost complementary source; directional, not statistically established." **Forbidden:** "significantly improved recall."

## 4. SASRec-small — sequence model
- **Problem:** predict next item from history order. **Intuition:** self-attention weights relevant past items. **Mechanism:** 1-block/1-head self-attentive (d48, maxlen30), last-position next-item. **Assumptions:** next-item is sequence-predictable; sufficient history.
- **Why here:** best sequence result; complements MF in the ranker. **Why not alternatives:** bigger SASRec/full-softmax didn't beat it on this data. **Evidence:** G11C/D (`g11cd_colab_metrics.json`); **numbers:** R@20 **0.0594** (GPU) / 0.036 (CPU) — below ALS 0.085. **Failure modes:** short histories (NaN on empty for 2-block variant), weak sequence signal. **If wrong:** over-trust a weaker generator. **Kill:** if it adds no ranker value. **Senior trap:** "undertrained?" → no, simpler config won. **Safe line:** "best sequence model, still below the MF floor." **Forbidden:** "sequence models beat MF."

## 5. Canonical SASRec — full-softmax vs sampled negatives
- **Problem:** is sampled-negative training the bottleneck? **Intuition:** full softmax sees all items as negatives → sharper gradients. **Mechanism:** d64/2blk full-position cross-entropy over all 40k vs 100 sampled negatives. **Assumptions:** GPU makes full softmax feasible.
- **Why here:** isolates the loss/objective lever. **Evidence:** G11C/D; **numbers:** sampled-neg **0.0068 → full-softmax 0.0456 (6.7×)**, still < ALS 0.085. **Failure modes:** 2-block + left-padding → NaN for short histories (3,397 users); not converged at 40 ep. **If wrong:** blame the architecture when the loss was the issue. **Kill:** n/a (diagnostic). **Senior trap:** "so full-softmax wins?" → it closes a 6.7× gap but not the gap to ALS. **Safe line:** "the loss mattered (6.7×), but it still didn't beat MF." **Forbidden:** "full-softmax SASRec is competitive with ALS."

## 6. GRU4Rec — recurrent sequence baseline
- **Problem:** sequence baseline distinct from attention. **Intuition:** GRU compresses history into a state. **Mechanism:** GRU over item embeddings, full-position. **Assumptions:** recurrence captures order.
- **Why here:** tournament breadth; second sequence family. **Evidence:** G11/G11C/D; **numbers:** R@20 0.0356 (GPU 30ep) / 0.0306 (CPU), below ALS and ≈ SASRec-small. **Failure modes:** same weak-sequence-signal ceiling. **If wrong:** over-weight a non-winning family. **Kill:** no ranker contribution. **Senior trap:** "why include it?" → honest tournament breadth, not a winner. **Safe line:** "recurrent baseline, same range as SASRec, below MF." **Forbidden:** "GRU4Rec is competitive."

## 7. TwoTower — neural retrieval (honest negative)
- **Problem:** learned two-tower retrieval (industry-standard). **Intuition:** user tower + item tower, dot-product retrieval. **Mechanism:** pooled-history user tower + item embedding, in-batch negatives. **Assumptions:** pooled history is an adequate user repr; item-ID-only (no content).
- **Why here:** industry-recognizable architecture; tested fairly. **Evidence:** G11D; **numbers:** R@20 **0.0020** (near random). **Failure modes:** mean-pool destroys sequence/structure on short histories; item-ID-only ≈ weak neural MF. **If wrong:** ship a near-random retriever. **Kill:** R@20 ≈ random → reject (we did, as a standalone retriever; kept only as a weak LTR feature). **Senior trap:** "two-tower is SOTA, why fail?" → item-ID pooled ≠ content/production two-tower; honest negative. **Safe line:** "I built a neural two-tower baseline; it was an honest negative on this data." **Forbidden:** "production-grade two-tower."

## 8. LambdaMART / LTR — ranker
- **Problem:** fuse many retriever signals into one ranking. **Intuition:** a learned ranker weighs ALS/content/popularity/sequence per context. **Mechanism:** LightGBM `lambdarank` (NDCG objective) over per-candidate features; user-grouped. **Assumptions:** candidate set contains the gold (coverage ceiling); features informative.
- **Why here:** the canonical two-stage rank layer; the material win. **Evidence:** G12-B/G13; **numbers:** ALS-rank **0.0382 → LTR 0.0791** (G12-B); mid **0.1201**. **Failure modes:** candidate-ceiling-bound; correlated features; overfit small relevance. **If wrong:** rank well over a candidate set that lacks the gold. **Kill:** if it can't beat single-retriever ordering. **Senior trap:** "within-candidate vs full-catalog?" → within-candidate, stated. **Safe line:** "LTR ~doubles single-retriever ranking within candidates." **Forbidden:** "full-catalog recall."

## 9. Candidate coverage / reachable-recall ceiling
- **Problem:** the ranker can't recover golds absent from candidates. **Intuition:** retrieval sets a hard ceiling; ranking only reorders within it. **Mechanism:** coverage@K = fraction of users whose gold ∈ candidate set. **Assumptions:** single held-out gold per user.
- **Why here:** the diagnostic that reframed the whole project. **Evidence:** G12-A/G13; **numbers:** G12 ceiling **0.157**, mid 0.323, wide 0.424; LTR captures ~half. **Failure modes:** mistaking ranking gains for end-to-end gains. **If wrong:** optimize ranking while recall is capped upstream. **Kill:** n/a (it's a measurement). **Senior trap:** "your R@20 looks low" → it's bounded by coverage, stated. **Safe line:** "end-to-end recall is retrieval-bound, not ranking-bound." **Forbidden:** "ranking fixed recall."

## 10. G13 candidate widening + knee decision
- **Problem:** lift the ceiling cost-effectively. **Intuition:** more/wider candidates raise coverage with diminishing returns. **Mechanism:** sweep ALS-N/SAS-N + content + pop; retrain LTR; find knee. **Assumptions:** candidate count ≈ serving cost.
- **Why here:** converts the G12-B diagnosis into a decision. **Evidence:** G13; **numbers:** coverage 0.157→0.42; R@20 0.086→**0.124** (+44%, ~3.2 SE); **knee = mid (~317 cands)** captures 0.120 of 0.124 max. **Failure modes:** within-set precision drops as candidates grow; cost. **If wrong:** pay 2× candidates for +0.004 R@20 (the wide trap). **Kill:** if widening doesn't lift R@20. **Senior trap:** "why not widest?" → reachable recall plateaued. **Safe line:** "widening converted; I adopted the knee, not the widest." **Forbidden:** "online recall gain."

## 11. G14 slate policy (pure LTR / MMR / cold reserve / creator cap / combined)
- **Problem:** assemble the final 20-slate under product/governance constraints. **Intuition:** relevance vs discovery/diversity/creator-fairness trade off. **Mechanism:** pure LTR; MMR diversity (λ); cold reserve (k); creator cap; combined. **Assumptions:** ≤10% warm-loss guardrail.
- **Why here:** the serving slate layer + governance test. **Evidence:** G14; **numbers:** pure LTR R@20 0.1201 (already cold 0.282); creator cap Gini 0.864→0.847 at **−9.1%**; **combined 20.7% self-violation**. **Failure modes:** greedy can't satisfy multi-constraints (needs constrained optimizer). **If wrong:** sacrifice relevance for marginal governance. **Kill:** governance ↑ but relevance collapses → reject (we did, except light cold reserve). **Senior trap:** "why no fancy governance policy?" → candidate stage already handled discovery. **Safe line:** "pure LTR default; governance best handled upstream." **Forbidden:** "solved creator fairness."

## 12. FAISS IndexFlatIP — exact serving index
- **Problem:** serve embedding retrieval fast. **Intuition:** optimized exact inner-product search beats naive matmul. **Mechanism:** flat IP index over ALS item factors. **Assumptions:** dense embeddings (ALS slice only).
- **Why here:** lossless latency win for serving. **Evidence:** G16; **numbers:** p95 **0.475 ms vs 0.781 brute**, retention **1.000**, 10.4 MB, 11.6k qps. **Failure modes:** cold items (no embedding) not indexable → content lane; users w/o factor → fallback (310). **If wrong:** assume ANN improves quality. **Kill:** n/a (exact). **Senior trap:** "does it improve recs?" → no, retains recall, cuts latency. **Safe line:** "exact FAISS — same results, ~39% lower p95." **Forbidden:** "FAISS improved recommendations."

## 13. FAISS IVF — approximation (scale-demo only)
- **Problem:** sub-linear retrieval at large catalogs. **Intuition:** cluster items, search few cells. **Mechanism:** IVF (nlist 256), nprobe sweep. **Assumptions:** approximation acceptable only if recall retention high.
- **Why here:** demonstrates the ANN-at-scale pattern. **Evidence:** G16; **numbers:** nprobe=64 retention@200 **0.872 (< 0.95)**; faster but lossy. **Failure modes:** aggressive nprobe collapses recall (0.13 at nprobe1). **If wrong:** ship lossy retrieval where exact is already fast. **Kill:** can't hit 0.95 retention at useful speed → not adopted (we didn't). **Senior trap:** "why not use ANN?" → 40k items too small; exact is sub-ms. **Safe line:** "IVF is a scale-demonstration, not a current-size win." **Forbidden:** "ANN sped up serving here."

## 14. OPE — IPS / SNIPS / DR (known-propensity simulator)
- **Problem:** estimate a new policy's value from logs of another. **Intuition:** reweight logged rewards by propensity ratios. **Mechanism:** IPS, self-normalized SNIPS, doubly-robust DR vs known truth in a simulator. **Assumptions:** known/overlapping propensities (positivity).
- **Why here:** offline A/B-readiness without touching users. **Evidence:** O1; **numbers:** ESS 1.4k→7.6k by logger; DR can't separate 90/10 from ALS. **Failure modes:** positivity failure → high variance/bias. **If wrong:** trust an unidentifiable policy comparison. **Kill:** ESS collapse → unreliable. **Senior trap:** "did OPE prove a winner?" → no, it bounds risk. **Safe line:** "OPE validated estimators and showed positivity is decisive." **Forbidden:** "OPE proved lift."

## 15. Position-bias correction
- **Problem:** logged clicks confound examination with relevance. **Intuition:** top slots get examined more regardless of quality. **Mechanism:** PBM examination model + IPS examination-correction. **Assumptions:** position propensities known/estimable.
- **Why here:** prevents killing the cold lane on a position artifact. **Evidence:** P1; **numbers:** naive CTR under-credits cold lane **~11×**; IPS recovers ~77% at aggregate. **Failure modes:** per-item IPS high variance; examination known only in sim. **If wrong:** kill good discovery on biased CTR. **Kill:** decisions on raw CTR → forbidden. **Senior trap:** "is raw CTR fine?" → no, 11× misread. **Safe line:** "raw CTR would have killed the right lane; position correction fixes it." **Forbidden:** "engagement measurement solved."

## 16. Prevalence-correct OPE + logger positivity
- **Problem:** 50/50 eval over-weights cold; logger choice gates OPE. **Intuition:** re-weight to real traffic; exploration determines reliability. **Mechanism:** prevalence re-weighting to 76.5/23.5; positivity/ESS diagnosis. **Assumptions:** known prevalence; structured exploration.
- **Why here:** realistic value + reliability gate. **Evidence:** O1b; **numbers:** field compresses; `dr_separates_90_10_from_als = False`. **Failure modes:** mismatched live prevalence (G3 risk B6). **If wrong:** overstate discovery value. **Kill:** unstable prevalence assumptions → hold. **Senior trap:** "so 90/10 wins?" → indistinguishable from control offline. **Safe line:** "prevalence-corrected; 90/10 is product-rational, not offline-proven." **Forbidden:** "90/10 proven better."

## 17. Creator/catalog health + exposure concentration
- **Problem:** does the policy widen the catalog or just concentrate? **Intuition:** reach ≠ de-concentration. **Mechanism:** exposure Gini, effective creators, long-tail, feedback-loop sim; creator-cap reranking. **Assumptions:** simple rich-get-richer loop; offline.
- **Why here:** catalog-health governance. **Evidence:** H1/H2; **numbers:** 90/10 +26% creators, +444 new, but Gini flat-to-worse; reranking de-concentrates only at −18% to −36% recall. **Failure modes:** mistaking reach for fairness. **If wrong:** claim fairness from reach. **Kill:** if de-concentration needs unacceptable relevance loss → defer to training-time. **Senior trap:** "did you fix fairness?" → no, reach not de-concentration. **Safe line:** "improved reach, not de-concentration; fairness needs a training-time fix." **Forbidden:** "creator fairness solved."

## 18. Claim-safety framework
- **Problem:** keep every claim bounded. **Intuition:** four axes of honesty. **Mechanism:** tag every result on offline-vs-online, synthetic-vs-real, within-candidate-vs-full-catalog, production-shaped-vs-production. **Assumptions:** consistent enforcement.
- **Why here:** the project's differentiator and its defense. **Evidence:** CLAIM1, ledger (docs/12,33), all gate reports. **Numbers:** every gate carries tag/N/CI-or-noise/source/reproducibility. **Failure modes:** silent scope drift (e.g., comparing within-candidate to full-catalog). **If wrong:** one overclaim sinks credibility. **Kill:** any unbounded claim → delete. **Senior trap:** "isn't this just a toy?" → real domain + governance + documented negatives. **Safe line:** "offline, evidence-backed, scope-tagged — nothing presented as proven lift or deployed." **Forbidden:** any of: online lift / production / cold-start solved / fairness solved / gold-complete.
